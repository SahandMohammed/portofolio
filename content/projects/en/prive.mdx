Privé Lounge is a premium men's grooming and wellness destination based in **Sulaymaniyah, Kurdistan Region, Iraq**. Its services include professional grooming, skincare, laser hair removal, and personal care treatments.

As part of **LogicBloom**, I worked on designing and implementing the technology ecosystem supporting Privé's operations, from its public-facing website and internal business management software to its cloud hosting infrastructure and deployment processes.

At the center of this project is **BloomERP for Privé**, a lightweight, industry-specific implementation of LogicBloom's proprietary BloomERP platform. Rather than introducing the complexity of a full enterprise resource planning system, this implementation focuses on practical business operations, connecting point-of-sale transactions, invoicing, inventory, payment processing, financial records, and professional performance reporting.

The project combines **full-stack software engineering, business process modeling, relational database design, Linux administration, cloud infrastructure, and DevOps** into a real-world business solution.

## The Challenge

Launching a premium grooming business requires more than a digital storefront. Daily operations involve multiple interconnected processes, from collecting payments for services to recording product consumption, managing inventory, tracking employee performance, and reconciling financial transactions.

Using disconnected software or spreadsheets for these processes creates unnecessary manual work and increases the risk of inconsistencies between operational and financial records.

Privé needed a centralized system that could:

- Process services and retail sales through a straightforward POS interface.
- Track payments and outstanding balances accurately.
- Manage products, warehouses, and internal material consumption.
- Associate service revenue with individual grooming professionals.
- Provide daily, weekly, and monthly operational reports.
- Restrict access to sensitive business functionality through permissions.
- Support English, Arabic, and Kurdish Sorani.
- Operate reliably from the lounge's computers and tablets.
- Support ongoing development without disrupting day-to-day operations.
- Establish a foundation for customer-facing appointments and mobile applications.

The primary architectural challenge was balancing **ERP-level transactional consistency with the simplicity expected from a service-oriented business application**.

## System Architecture

The ecosystem consists of multiple application layers with distinct responsibilities.

**Public Website:** A Next.js application hosted on Vercel, providing the business's branded online presence and multilingual content.

**ERP Frontend:** A React and TypeScript application responsible for administrative operations, point-of-sale workflows, and operational reporting.

The public website can evolve independently from the management application, while transactional rules remain centralized in the backend rather than duplicated across client applications.

This separation provides a foundation for future applications to reuse the same core operational services.

## BloomERP for Privé — A Lightweight ERP Implementation

BloomERP is LogicBloom's proprietary enterprise resource planning and business management platform.

For Privé, the platform was adapted into a **lightweight, specialized implementation**, retaining the architectural foundations of an ERP while concentrating on the features necessary for daily operations.

This approach avoids introducing unrelated enterprise modules into a relatively focused service business.

The implementation centers around several connected domains:

- Point of Sale
- Sales and Purchase Invoicing
- Payment Processing
- Money Accounts and Financial Records
- Inventory and Warehouse Management
- Internal Product Consumption
- Professionals and Performance Reporting
- User Management and Access Control
- Localization and Operational Reporting

An important engineering principle throughout the implementation was to **reuse shared business concepts instead of introducing separate implementations for every interface or workflow**.

### Unified Sales Architecture

One of the most important architectural decisions was consolidating POS transactions and manually entered sales invoices into a single commercial document model.

Rather than maintaining separate entities and accounting logic for POS receipts and conventional sales, both workflows use the underlying `SalesInvoice` domain.

Every sales document participates in the same numbering system, following a consistent format such as `SINV-000001`.

POS transactions retain additional contextual information without becoming an entirely separate financial model.

This includes metadata such as:

- Associated sales invoice
- Cashier identification
- Completion timestamp
- Client request identifier
- Request fingerprint

The request metadata also provides a foundation for identifying repeated submissions and protecting transaction-processing workflows.

<Callout title="Architecture Decision — One Source of Truth">
  POS is treated as a specialized workflow for creating and settling a Sales
  Invoice, not an independent sales or accounting subsystem. This allows
  reporting, payment calculations, invoice corrections, and financial
  reconciliation to operate against the same underlying commercial records.
</Callout>

### Sessionless POS Design

Traditional retail POS systems often rely on register-opening procedures, cashier sessions, and separate end-of-day closing documents.

For Privé's operational model, this would introduce unnecessary complexity.

The implementation therefore uses a sessionless POS design. Transactions are posted directly as ordinary sales invoices, with the relevant cashier and payment information recorded through the existing business domains.

Refunds and transaction corrections preserve their own audit-related information without depending on register sessions.

The result is a simpler operational experience while preserving the financial records required by the system.

## Centralized Payments & Financial Integrity

A core engineering challenge in business management applications is ensuring that invoice state accurately reflects money movement.

For example, changing an invoice after payment must not silently modify historical payment records or create a false impression that the transaction remains fully settled.

BloomERP addresses this through a centralized payment and allocation architecture.

### Payment Domain

The payment model includes several related concepts:

- **Payment:** Represents a recorded payment transaction.
- **PaymentAllocation:** Connects a payment to the commercial document or balance it settles.
- **PaymentMoneyLine:** Represents money movement through the selected payment accounts.
- **MoneyLedger:** Maintains corresponding financial account movement records.

Rather than relying on a manually maintained invoice status, payment status is derived from the relationship between the commercial amount and recorded financial allocations.

The system distinguishes:

| Status  | Meaning                                                         |
| ------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Unpaid  | No payment has been allocated                                   |
| Partial | A portion of the balance remains outstanding                    |
| Credit  | An outstanding amount is associated with an identified customer |
| Paid    | The required amount has been settled                            |

The model enforces additional rules based on transaction type.

For example, ordinary walk-in transactions must be fully paid, while outstanding balances require an identified customer.

### Commercial Corrections Without Rewriting Payment History

Consider an invoice originally issued for **25,000 IQD** and fully paid.

If the commercial invoice is later corrected to **30,000 IQD**, the system does not automatically alter the historical payment to match the new amount.

Instead, the existing 25,000 IQD payment remains recorded, and the invoice reflects the additional 5,000 IQD outstanding balance.

This distinction prevents commercial document changes from implicitly rewriting financial transactions.

### Reversal Behavior

The payment architecture also considers deletion and correction workflows.

For example, removing a posted POS transaction requires corresponding payment reversals or appropriate soft-deletion behavior so that financial balances remain consistent.

<Callout title="Engineering Highlight — Financial Consistency">
  Commercial documents and money movements have distinct responsibilities.
  Invoice corrections change commercial obligations, while payment allocations
  and ledger records determine financial settlement. This separation helps
  prevent inconsistent balances and hidden accounting side effects.
</Callout>

## Inventory & Warehouse Management

Privé's operations require inventory control for both products sold to customers and materials consumed internally while delivering services.

A traditional retail-only inventory model would not adequately represent this distinction.

The lightweight BloomERP implementation therefore supports inventory workflows spanning:

- Product catalog management
- Warehouse-based stock tracking
- Purchase-related inventory movements
- Sales-related stock movements
- Product usage and internal consumption
- Units of measurement
- Stock valuation
- Operational stock exceptions

### Internal Usage Invoices

An important domain-specific feature is the **Usage Invoice**.

Usage invoices allow the business to record products consumed internally without creating a customer sale.

For example, a grooming product or consumable used by a professional during a service reduces available stock, but does not represent a separately billed retail transaction.

The implementation supports:

- Manual usage invoice creation and editing.
- Source warehouse selection for individual lines.
- Default warehouse selection for simplified data entry.
- Product and unit selection.
- Immediate posting behavior.
- Inventory reconciliation when documents are edited.
- Reversal of stock effects when applicable documents are deleted.

This connects internal operational consumption to the inventory records.

### Weighted-Average Costing

Inventory valuation uses weighted-average costing principles.

The system maintains cost information associated with stock movements rather than treating all inventory adjustments as quantity-only operations.

### Configurable Negative Stock

Some businesses need to continue operating even when physical inventory records are temporarily incomplete.

The architecture supports configurable negative-stock handling at the branch level.

Where negative stock is permitted, the system associates resulting cost differences with a configured variance account.

Inventory transfers and physical counts retain their own restrictions to prevent the negative-stock option from becoming an unrestricted bypass of inventory controls.

This offers operational flexibility while preserving the corresponding financial implications.

## Professional Performance & Operational Reporting

Unlike conventional retail businesses, Privé's revenue is substantially tied to services performed by individual grooming professionals.

The ERP therefore includes professional attribution for service transactions.

Service lines are associated with a professional, while ordinary product sales do not require that association.

### Performance Metrics

The reporting system supports analysis across different periods and operational dimensions.

Available reporting dimensions include:

- Today
- This Week
- This Month
- Custom date range
- Individual professional
- Service
- Transaction source, including POS and manual sales

The reporting model includes service quantities, distinct customers, gross sales, refunds, and net sales.

Refund reporting is associated with the applicable posting period, providing a more consistent representation of transactions within the selected reporting window.

### Adapting Software to Real Employee Behavior

One of the most valuable lessons from the project emerged after the system was introduced into daily operations.

The original service model offered more detailed classification than the grooming professionals needed or wanted to record during busy working hours.

Instead of requiring employees to classify every service in detail, we simplified the workflow through configurable service price tiers.

This reduced the effort required to enter transactions while retaining the professional-level sales information management needed.

<Callout title="Product Engineering Lesson">
  Real-world software must adapt to the people operating it. A technically
  comprehensive workflow is not necessarily a better workflow. Operational
  feedback led to a simpler transaction experience without abandoning the
  underlying reporting and financial architecture.
</Callout>

## Reusable Frontend Architecture

The management application is built using **React, TypeScript, and Vite**, with Tailwind CSS and shadcn/ui providing the foundation for reusable interface elements.

A major engineering focus was avoiding independently constructed screens for every commercial document.

ERP applications frequently contain similar patterns across sales, purchases, usage documents, payments, and reports.

Without architectural discipline, these patterns become duplicated implementations with inconsistent behavior.

### Shared Document Components

The frontend uses common building blocks around document management workflows, including concepts such as:

- `DocumentWorkspace`
- `DocumentFields`
- `DocumentLinesTable`
- Shared document header and action patterns
- Common form and selection controls
- Shared payment and allocation interfaces

These components provide a consistent structure while allowing individual business modules to supply their own data and rules.

### Feature-Oriented Organization

Application functionality is grouped around business features rather than relying exclusively on generic component folders.

Business-specific behavior remains within its corresponding feature, while genuinely reusable presentation and infrastructure components are extracted into shared layers.

This architecture aims to balance reuse with clear ownership.

### Consistent Data Management

TanStack Query is used for frontend server-state management and cache behavior.

Specific optimization work includes POS catalog freshness, query invalidation, and management of frequently accessed data.

The objective is to keep user interactions responsive without compromising the accuracy of operational data.

### Engineering Governance

The development workflow also incorporates project architecture guidance, coding conventions, reusable frontend patterns, and AI-assisted implementation practices.

These rules help reduce architectural drift, duplicated abstractions, and inconsistent frontend implementations as the application evolves.

## CI/CD & Automated Deployment

Application delivery was another central engineering component of the project.

Instead of manually building and publishing every backend release from a development workstation, LogicBloom established a repeatable container image workflow.

### Delivery Pipeline

The deployment process uses:

1. **GitHub** for source control and repository management.
2. **GitHub Actions** for automated build and publishing workflows.
3. **.NET container publishing** targeting the ARM64 deployment environment.
4. **GitHub Container Registry (GHCR)** for image distribution.
5. **Oracle Cloud Infrastructure** for application runtime hosting.
6. **Docker Compose** for container configuration and lifecycle management.
7. **Caddy** for HTTPS ingress and application routing.

### ARM64 Compatibility

Because the production host uses an ARM-based processor, backend images must be compatible with Linux ARM64.

The automated build pipeline produces deployable artifacts targeting the server's processor architecture.

This avoids relying on incompatible development-machine images or manual compilation on the production server.

### Server-Side Deployment

The deployment design allows the server to retrieve published application images and update its running containers.

Application-specific Docker Compose definitions, environment configuration, and secrets are organized within the server's deployment directory structure.

The infrastructure includes health-check support and deployment-management procedures.

### Why This Matters

Automating the delivery process improves repeatability and reduces the amount of manual work required to deploy changes.

More importantly, it creates a reusable DevOps foundation for additional applications developed under LogicBloom.

## Significant Engineering Decisions

### 1. Extending BloomERP Instead of Building a Disconnected Application

The system reuses the architecture and business concepts of LogicBloom's broader ERP product while customizing the interface and workflows for Privé.

This avoids developing a separate solution with unrelated commercial and accounting rules.

### 2. One Sales Invoice Domain for POS and Manual Sales

A unified domain reduces duplication and provides consistent treatment of sales, payment allocation, and reporting.

### 3. Financial Records Are Not Automatically Rewritten by Commercial Corrections

Invoice revisions and payment changes are handled as distinct operations, preserving a clearer financial history.

### 4. Internal Consumption Is Not a Customer Sale

Usage invoices allow the system to represent stock consumed during service delivery without creating artificial retail transactions.

### 5. Business Feedback Takes Priority Over Unnecessary Workflow Complexity

Detailed service classifications were simplified after professionals indicated that fast transaction entry and daily totals were more useful to their operations.

### 6. Reusable Frontend Components Without Excessive Abstraction

Shared document components are used where workflows genuinely overlap, while feature-specific behavior remains within its domain.

### 7. Application Hosting and Database Hosting Are Separated

Containerized compute services run on Oracle Cloud, while PostgreSQL is managed through Neon.

### 8. Public Website and Internal ERP Have Independent Deployment Environments

This keeps the brand website from becoming tightly coupled to internal transactional application releases.

## Challenges & Lessons Learned

Working on a deployed business application presents challenges that do not always appear in purely academic or experimental software projects.

### Transactional Integrity

Financial and inventory workflows must remain correct when documents are edited, deleted, refunded, or partially paid.

This required careful consideration of related records and side effects rather than relying on simplistic CRUD operations.

### Operational Usability

A workflow can be logically correct but still be unsuitable for daily use.

Feedback from Privé's staff reinforced the importance of reducing unnecessary interactions, especially in high-frequency POS operations.

### Software Modularity

ERP applications contain many visually similar pages but different business rules.

Developing reusable interface patterns required balancing consistency with clear domain boundaries.

### Cloud Deployment

Running a modern ASP.NET Core application on a small ARM64 Linux instance required container compatibility, secure networking, and a repeatable deployment process.

### Evolving Requirements

The requirements continued to change after the initial business launch.

Maintaining extensible domain models and predictable component boundaries was important for introducing changes without rewriting unrelated features.
