**BloomERP** is LogicBloom's proprietary business management and enterprise resource planning platform. The product is designed to connect everyday business operations within a coherent software environment, with capabilities spanning commercial documents, inventory, payments, reporting, and administrative workflows.

My work combines requirements analysis, software architecture, full-stack implementation, and ongoing refinement of the platform. The focus is not simply building individual screens, but creating a maintainable product that can be adapted to different operating environments.

## Product Direction & Requirements

ERP software has to accommodate interconnected processes without making everyday tasks unnecessarily difficult. Requirements are gathered from practical business scenarios, translated into focused workflows, and evaluated against the existing platform before new functionality is introduced.

A recurring challenge is deciding what belongs in the reusable product and what should remain specific to an individual client's operations. This distinction guides feature boundaries, interface design, and decisions about extensibility.

<Callout title="Product Engineering">
  BloomERP is developed as a reusable business platform. Privé Lounge is one of its real-world, lightweight implementations—not the definition of the entire product.
</Callout>

## Full-Stack Development

The platform's implementation work uses **ASP.NET Core and C#** for backend services, **PostgreSQL** for relational persistence, and **React with TypeScript** for the management experience. The frontend uses reusable UI elements and shared interaction patterns to support consistency as the application grows.

Engineering responsibilities include organizing business functionality into maintainable modules, integrating administrative workflows with backend services, and refining common document interfaces. Transactional correctness and an understandable user experience are important considerations, particularly where inventory, commercial documents, and financial information intersect.

## Architecture & Maintainability

An ERP evolves continuously. As new operational requirements emerge, its architecture must remain clear enough to accommodate them without duplicating the same behavior across unrelated modules.

My approach emphasizes reusable components where workflows genuinely overlap, clear ownership of business-specific functionality, and incremental improvements informed by usage and review. The objective is to maintain an extensible platform without introducing abstractions that add more complexity than value.

## Real-World Application

**BloomERP for Privé** demonstrates how the broader platform can be tailored into a lightweight operational system. That implementation includes an integrated point-of-sale experience, business documents, inventory, and professional reporting, supported by LogicBloom's application infrastructure.

The product continues to evolve. This case study intentionally describes the public-facing engineering scope without disclosing proprietary domain rules, internal configuration, or implementation details.
