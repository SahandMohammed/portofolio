The **Jalal Talabani International Airport (JTIA)** project brought together public-facing web development and supporting administration workflows within an aviation context.

Working on the platform involved more than presenting static information. Airport content changes over time, and publishing relevant information requires coordination between public interfaces, structured application data, and existing operational sources.

## Understanding the Requirements

The project required considering two different audiences: airport visitors looking for clear, accessible information and authorized staff responsible for maintaining that information. This distinction influenced the separation between the public website and its supporting management capabilities.

Requirements included structured treatment of flight-related information and airport content, together with the ability to manage data through a consistent administrative experience. A central consideration was how the web platform should relate to information already maintained elsewhere in the airport environment.

## Application Stack

The technical foundation combines **Next.js** for the web application and **NestJS** for backend services, using **TypeScript** across the stack and **PostgreSQL** for relational data.

The broader development setup used a shared-code workspace to improve consistency across application boundaries. Database access and application delivery were supported by established tooling, including TypeORM and Docker-based deployment work.

<Callout title="Systems Integration">
  The project involved planning how airport web information could connect with existing operational data rather than treating every piece of information as a manually maintained standalone record.
</Callout>

## Building for a Public-Facing Environment

An airport website needs information architecture that works for a wide range of visitors. Development considerations included readable content, navigation, responsive layouts, and administrative tools appropriate for frequently changing data.

The platform's scope included flight information, airlines, destinations, publications, and news-related content, alongside supporting administrative functionality. My engineering work focused on the relationship between the user-facing experience, the backend services, and the data those services expose.

## Delivery & Lessons

The project also involved application packaging and deployment work in a containerized environment. That experience reinforced the importance of keeping application development, systems integration, and operational delivery aligned.

JTIA demonstrates full-stack development in a domain where accurate information presentation and integration with existing systems are important. Internal interfaces, infrastructure configuration, and operational details are intentionally excluded from this public case study.
